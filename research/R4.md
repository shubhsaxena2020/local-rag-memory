# R4: Evidence-grounded answers and calibrated abstention

**Status:** Codex-authored evidence-based replacement after R4 exhausted four worker attempts without an acceptable report. Literature review only; no local model, answerer, judge, prompt, or pipeline experiment was run. Evidence checked 2026-10-19.

## Recommendation

Start with a simple answer contract: retrieve evidence first; answer only claims supported by the retrieved sources; attach a source to each material claim; state conflicts and dates; abstain when evidence is absent or materially ambiguous. Test this rule against the current prompt with a fixed answerer and the same questions. Do not treat a stronger instruction as an accuracy guarantee.

Measure support at claim level, relative both to retrieved evidence and to the known source of truth. These are different tests: a response can be faithful to a wrong retrieved passage, or factually correct from parametric memory while unsupported by the returned evidence. Report both. Report answerable false-refusal separately from no-answer precision and recall so an abstention fix cannot improve one headline by refusing everything.

Keep retrieval-quality checks separate from generation faithfulness. A claim verifier can flag likely unsupported spans and offer a cited revision, but automated decomposition and entailment are fallible. Require a human-checked calibration set and an explicit abstain path. There is no universal effect size or laptop cost established by the reviewed literature for this project's corpus and answerer.

## What the evidence says

### Retrieval and generation should be controlled separately

- Self-RAG trains a 7B/13B model to retrieve adaptively and use reflection tokens to critique retrieved evidence and generated text. Its paper reports gains over ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama 2 Chat on its open-domain QA, reasoning, fact-verification, and long-form citation/factuality tasks. Those are task- and model-specific results; they do not establish that a prompt-only change to this project's fixed Sonnet answerer will have the same effect. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511).
- Corrective RAG (CRAG) evaluates retrieved-document quality and routes among actions such as refining/filtering retrieved evidence or augmenting with web search; the paper evaluates its design across four datasets. The method assumes access to a retrieval evaluator and sometimes a web source, so it has additional inference/tool cost and a different threat surface. No transferable project effect size is inferred here. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15884).
- FaithfulRAG explicitly models fact-level conflicts between parametric knowledge and retrieved context. This supports treating disagreement as a first-class case rather than blanket-suppressing all parametric knowledge. Its published benchmark results are not a local result and do not justify adopting its added reasoning path without a matched ablation. [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1062/).

### Claim-level evaluation is more diagnostic than one answer score

- FActScore breaks long-form generations into atomic facts and measures the share supported by a specified knowledge source. The original paper's human study reported a 58% FActScore for ChatGPT on its biography task, showing how answer-level impressions can hide unsupported subclaims. This number is not a baseline for memory Q&A. Its authors also describe their automatic estimator and note assumptions about source verifiability; estimates need validation on the target domain. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251).
- RAGTruth contains nearly 18,000 RAG responses with manual case- and word-level hallucination annotations across QA, summarization, and data-to-text. It is useful to develop or stress-test detectors, not a direct score for this project's conversational memory task. Its source corpora and release terms need their own review before reuse. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00396), [official repository](https://github.com/ParticleMedia/RAGTruth).
- RAGChecker proposes fine-grained metrics that separate retrieval context quality, context utilization, and response faithfulness. Its paper uses a specific benchmark of 4,162 queries across 10 domains and evaluates selected systems/judges; its benchmark and judge must not be confused with local labeled truth. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08067).
- RAGVUE (EACL 2026 demo) separates retrieval quality, answer relevance/completeness, claim-level faithfulness, and judge calibration. This decomposition is useful for diagnostics, but an automated framework still requires validation against human annotations. [ACL Anthology paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-demo.35/).

### Automated judges and confidence need calibration

- ARES evaluates context relevance, answer faithfulness, and answer relevance with trained lightweight judges and uses a small human-labeled sample with prediction-powered inference (PPI) to account for prediction errors. The paper evaluates eight tasks across KILT, SuperGLUE, and AIS and reports use of a few hundred human annotations. Its key transferable point is calibration against human judgments, not treating its raw judge score as ground truth. [NAACL 2024 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.20/).
- RAGAS provides a metric framework for faithfulness and related RAG properties; it is not one fixed gold dataset and LLM-based metrics depend on a judge. Keep metric names, prompts, judge model, version, and calibration data with every result. [EACL 2024 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-demo.16/), [official metrics documentation](https://docs.ragas.io/en/latest/concepts/metrics/available_metrics/).
- “Geometry-Calibrated Conformal Abstention for Language Models” (2026 arXiv preprint) proposes a post-hoc conformal abstention approach and reports selective answering with 75% conditional correctness in its evaluation. The reported value is benchmark/model-specific and not the same as 90% no-answer precision. Conformal guarantees depend on calibration/evaluation assumptions; they do not automatically transfer to shifting conversational memory queries. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27914).
- Standard selective-prediction tradeoff applies: raising a refusal threshold can improve precision among answered items while reducing coverage and increasing false refusals. Evaluate the full coverage-risk curve and report answerable false-abstention rate, no-answer precision, and no-answer recall at the chosen operating point.

## Ranked techniques for this product

Ranking is by expected diagnostic value and implementation cost, not by a pooled cross-paper effect size.

| Rank | Technique | Evidence and expected value | Cost / limitation | Recommendation |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evidence-first answer format with source IDs/quotes | Cheap way to expose whether evidence was actually present; supports claim-level review. Related retrieval-grounded methods are evaluated by Self-RAG/CRAG, but a prompt-only effect is unknown. | Low code cost; may reduce answer completeness/fluency; a quote can still be irrelevant or misread. | TEST first with fixed answerer and matched questions. |
| 2 | Claim decomposition plus claim-to-source checks | FActScore, RAGTruth, RAGChecker, and RAGVUE motivate claim/span granularity and separate support from answer-level judgments. | Extra extraction/check pass; errors can cascade; needs human-labeled calibration. | TEST on small paired sample, then expand only if judge agreement is adequate. |
| 3 | Conflict-aware generation | FaithfulRAG supports representing source/parametric conflict explicitly rather than ignoring it. Memory-specific conflict must also use fact validity and provenance. | Additional reasoning/context; model can choose the wrong side. | TEST on explicit conflict cases; never let the answerer rewrite source truth. |
| 4 | Retrieval quality gate / corrective retrieval | CRAG proposes grading evidence and modifying retrieval when evidence is poor. Can reduce answering from weak context if the evaluator is calibrated. | Additional model/tool call; web augmentation adds SSRF, freshness, source-quality, and latency risks. | TEST local evidence grading first; keep web retrieval out of this local-memory product unless separately threat-modeled. |
| 5 | Calibrated abstention / selective prediction | Conformal abstention provides a principled calibration direction and an explicit risk/coverage tradeoff. | Requires representative calibration labels; distribution shift invalidates naive guarantees; over-refusal is a real failure. | TEST only after answerable and no-answer sets are independently labeled. |
| 6 | Self-consistency or repeated sampling | Can reveal instability in some tasks, but agreement is not evidence and multiplies generation cost. | Multiple answer calls; correlation between samples; may amplify a shared error. | REJECT as a standalone confidence score; only test as an ablation if budget permits. |
| 7 | NLI/entailment verifier | Potentially cheaper than full generation if a suitable model exists; checks claim/evidence pairs. | Domain and negation/date errors; another model must be calibrated; false negatives can cause over-refusal. | TEST as an optional verify path, not a mandatory gate until measured. |

## Proposed local experiment

**Question:** does an evidence-first answer instruction reduce unsupported claims without creating unacceptable false refusals?

**Conditions:** (A) current frozen answer prompt; (B) answer only from the retrieved evidence, cite each factual claim, report conflicting/dated evidence, and abstain only when support is absent/ambiguous. Optional condition (C) adds a claim-level verifier only if E11's implementation is ready. Use isolated folders; the no-memory baseline must not see retrieved facts or gold answers.

**Fixed factors:** one exact answerer model for every condition and every comparison row; record provider/model/revision, prompt hash, temperature, retrieval snapshot, and token limits. The Round 3 primary design says Sonnet 5.5, but the actual quota-selected answerer must be fixed for the whole comparison; if it cannot run consistently, postpone the comparison rather than mixing models.

**Data:** corrected non-sealed dev set for tuning; held-out set for one confirmation. Include answerable, conflicting/stale, and no-answer probes. Keep SEALED unopened until the final one-time scoring phase.

**Labels:** independent human labels for each atomic answer claim as supported, unsupported, contradicted, or not assessable relative to (1) returned evidence and (2) source-of-truth labels. Record answer-level refusal separately. A judge may assist only after calibration, two-family overlap, kappa, and a hand-read sample per plan section 5.4.

**Primary metrics:** unsupported-claim rate with memory; contradicted-claim rate; no-answer abstention precision/recall; answerable false-abstention rate; answer coverage; source citation precision; latency and token cost. Report exact denominators and bootstrap 95% CIs. For paired prompt changes, resample questions as pairs.

**Decision rule:** retain the grounding change only if the paired held-out reduction in unsupported claims is supported by its confidence interval and answerable false-abstention/coverage do not worsen beyond the preregistered bounds. Calibrate any abstention threshold on dev only. Do not retune on held-out or SEALED.

**Budget:** prompt-only comparison adds no local verifier call; measure API token and latency cost. A verifier adds at least one check per answer or claim batch; actual local CPU/RAM/latency and quality cost are UNKNOWN until measured. No numeric latency or quality gain is asserted here.

## ADOPT / TEST / REJECT

- **ADOPT (evaluation practice):** report claim-level support and contradiction separately from retrieval recall; keep answerable false refusals visible; calibrate automated judges against hand-labeled examples; keep the answerer constant within a comparison.
- **TEST:** evidence-first prompt, claim-to-source verification, explicit conflict handling, calibrated abstention, and a gated verifier. Run the fixed-answerer experiment above.
- **REJECT:** claiming zero hallucination from a prompt or judge; using self-consistency as truth; using an uncalibrated LLM confidence score as a safety guarantee; setting the refusal threshold on held-out/sealed data; combining runs from different answerer models in one comparison; blanket web correction without a separate security design.

## Limitations

- Published effect sizes differ in task, model, source collection, judge, annotation unit, and metric; they are not pooled or transferred to this repository.
- The reviewed evidence does not prove a cheap verifier is reliable on local memory facts or that quote-only answers improve quality for this exact answerer.
- All local costs, gains, calibration errors, and judge agreement remain UNKNOWN until measured.
- The exhausted R4 worker draft contained untraceable numeric gains and arbitrary thresholds; none were accepted into this report.
